Your ready-to-run brief

Six decisions before you ask an agent to build

Write enough that someone else could tell what to build, what to avoid, and how to test it. Examples show a trainee onboarding navigator; replace them with your own idea. Text entered here stays in this browser session until you copy or download it; this page does not submit it to a server.

Who needs the tool, when, and what should they accomplish?

Example: A new practicum student finds the next onboarding activity and its source link.

What materials or rules should the agent use? What if they are incomplete?

Example: Use a cleared sample checklist. Preserve timing and flag missing links.

What are the two or three essential things it should do?

Example: Show this week's tasks, source links, and a progress check.

What must it avoid collecting, inferring, claiming, or publishing?

Example: No patient data or trainee evaluations; do not generate new clinical guidance.

What safe example will show whether it works? What would make you revise it?

Example: A Week 1 user sees Week 1 tasks; progress survives refresh.

Who checks the result before anyone else uses it?

Example: A supervisor checks source coverage and links; a trainee tests usability.

Attach the approved source separately when you run the brief. Avoid entering confidential material in this page or an unapproved tool.

After the workshop

Hand it off, then expect a revision

What the copied brief asks an agent to do

Inspect the supplied source; flag missing or conflicting material; build the smallest working version within your boundaries; run your acceptance test; report what passed, failed, or could not be checked; and explain the result. It asks before publishing, connecting accounts, using sensitive data, or taking consequential actions.

1. Try your testUse the safe scenario you wrote, not a real patient case.
2. Check source and claimsFind what the agent preserved, omitted, or invented.
3. Request one revisionName the behavior that failed and what success looks like.
4. Retest and reviewKeep the brief, source, and revision notes together.

A working preview is a prototype. Patient-facing content, clinical claims, and local workflows need appropriate evidence and human review before use.
